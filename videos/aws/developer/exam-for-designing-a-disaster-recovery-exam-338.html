The correct design is Cloud Spanner multi region configuration with synchronous replication and scheduled exports every 10 minutes.

This design provides strongly consistent writes across regions using synchronous replication, which means committed transactions are already durable in multiple regions. That gives you an effective RPO of zero for committed data and easily satisfies an RPO target of 20 minutes. Automatic failover between regions is built into the service and recovery can be achieved within minutes under normal conditions, which is well inside a 45 minute RTO. Scheduled exports every 10 minutes add an additional recovery method for accidental deletion or corruption scenarios and they further ensure that point in time recovery options are always current.

Cloud SQL with asynchronous cross region replicas and automated exports every 20 minutes is not appropriate because asynchronous replication can lag and offers no strict upper bound on data loss during a regional failure, so you cannot reliably meet a 20 minute RPO. Promotion and potential restore workflows also risk exceeding a 45 minute RTO, and exporting every 20 minutes does not change the asynchronous nature of replication or the restore time.

Cloud Spanner regional configuration with scheduled exports every 10 minutes does not meet the cross regional requirement because a regional instance has no second region to fail over to. Meeting the RTO would require provisioning and restoring into another region from exports, which is likely to exceed 45 minutes even though the export cadence could satisfy a 20 minute RPO target on paper.

Bigtable with multi cluster routing in two regions and hourly backups fails the RPO requirement because hourly backups allow up to one hour of data loss. Multi cluster routing provides eventual consistency across clusters rather than the strongly consistent cross regional writes that are needed to confidently meet a 20 minute RPO for transactional workloads.
