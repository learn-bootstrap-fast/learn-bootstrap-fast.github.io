The correct option is The costs for query execution and index maintenance.

This is the most workload driven and comparable component across managed NoSQL services. Queries consume billable operations or capacity and the structure and selectivity of queries determine how many reads or how much compute is required. Indexes improve query performance but they increase write amplification and storage, and they can require ongoing maintenance that directly affects spend. Emphasizing these costs allows an apples to apples comparison because they scale with traffic patterns and data access rather than with vendor specific features.

In Google Cloud services such as Firestore, billing is tied to document reads, writes, deletes, storage and index storage, so query patterns and indexing choices materially shift monthly cost. In Bigtable, you pay for provisioned capacity and storage which is sized to satisfy read and write throughput driven by your query access patterns. Focusing on how queries execute and how indexes are built and maintained provides the most precise TCO estimate for an event tracking system.

The database's compatibility with third party tools and services can influence developer productivity and integration effort, yet it is not a direct or consistently measurable cost driver for TCO. It varies by team and use case and does not allow a precise monetary comparison between products.

Customer managed encryption keys for data at rest is primarily a security and compliance choice. While CMEK can introduce modest Cloud KMS usage charges and management overhead, these are usually small compared to the operational costs driven by queries and indexes and therefore should not be the emphasis for a precise cost comparison.

The overall popularity of the NoSQL product does not correlate with your actual spend. Popularity is not a cost component and offers no quantitative basis for comparing TCO.
