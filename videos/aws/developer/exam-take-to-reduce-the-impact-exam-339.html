The correct options are Configure a weekly maintenance window during off peak hours to defer patching to a quieter period and Subscribe to Cloud SQL maintenance notifications to receive alerts before work begins.

Using a maintenance window gives you control over when Cloud SQL applies updates that can restart the instance. Choosing a quiet period reduces the risk that customers will experience errors or latency. Cloud SQL defers eligible maintenance to the weekly window which lets the team plan connection draining, coordinate releases, and adjust traffic patterns.

Enabling maintenance notifications provides early warning of planned work and expected timing. With advance notice the operations team can prepare runbooks, notify stakeholders, and take preemptive steps such as scaling read replicas or routing traffic to minimize user impact.

Implement a connection pool between the application and the database to recycle sessions improves efficiency and connection management but it does not prevent restarts or failovers during maintenance and it does not provide advance notice, so it does not directly minimize scheduled maintenance impact.

Apply client retry logic with exponential backoff on database errors is a good resiliency pattern for transient errors, yet scheduled maintenance can involve longer restarts and connection resets. Retries alone do not control timing or provide early notice, so they do not address the core requirement.

Remove the maintenance window so updates are applied as soon as they are available reduces your control and can allow disruptive updates to occur during peak traffic. This increases risk rather than minimizing it.
