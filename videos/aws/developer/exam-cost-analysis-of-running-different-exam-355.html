The correct answer is Aligning instance classes and service pricing tiers across the databases.

This is the first thing to do because each managed database offers different unit economics and capacity abstractions. Cloud Spanner uses nodes or processing units, AlloyDB uses primary and read pool nodes, and Cloud SQL uses machine types and storage classes. If you do not first normalize the compute shape, memory, and storage tier across services then any regional or workload assumptions will skew the comparison. Establishing equivalent capacity and pricing tiers creates an apples to apples baseline for an accurate projection. After this alignment you can add regional pricing, network, storage growth, and high availability choices.

Chosen region and whether resources are regional or multi regional is important for final costing yet it is not the first step to ensure fairness. Region pricing varies and service capabilities differ across regions, and some products support multi region in different ways, so starting here can mask mismatches in capacity between services and lead to a misleading comparison.

Projected scalability goals and latency or throughput targets inform architecture and sizing, but they should be applied after you have normalized instance classes and tiers. Otherwise performance goals might drive different shapes that are not comparable and you would not achieve a fair baseline.

Expected network egress and any cross region replication traffic often affects the total bill, but it should be layered on once you have matched service tiers and capacity. Egress differs by region and topology and can distort the comparison if you have not first aligned the underlying instances.
