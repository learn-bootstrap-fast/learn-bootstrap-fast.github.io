The correct step is to read every block on EBS volumes restored from snapshots to pre-warm them before running your tests. Amazon EBS volumes restored from snapshots are lazy-loaded, meaning that only data requested is loaded on first access, causing initial read operations to experience higher latency. By pre-warming (reading all blocks), you ensure that your I/O performance test measures the actual, steady-state performance of the volume, not the initial slowness caused by lazy loading.

Why other options are incorrect:

  • Randomize the selection of I/O block sizes in each test run: This introduces variability, which makes your performance tests less repeatable. Consistency in test parameters is vital to obtain reliable results.
  • Back up EBS volumes by creating snapshots prior to each test: While snapshotting is useful for data backup, it does not impact the I/O performance characteristics of the volume during testing, nor does it resolve issues related to lazy loading.
  • Encrypt the underlying EBS volume before testing to ensure stability: Encryption does not guarantee consistent I/O performance, and it is not required for accurate performance benchmarking unless part of your workflow involves encrypted volumes specifically.

Tip: For accurate and repeatable I/O performance tests with EBS volumes, always ensure volumes are fully initialized and pre-warmed prior to running benchmarks, especially after restoring from a snapshot.
