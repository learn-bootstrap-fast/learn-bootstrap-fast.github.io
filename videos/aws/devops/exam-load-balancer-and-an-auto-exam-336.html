The best solution is to deploy a CloudWatch alarm based on the NetworkIn metric of the EC2 instances within your Auto Scaling group, and set up Amazon SNS to inform your team via alert notifications. This approach provides near real-time monitoring (CloudWatch alarms often check every 1–5 minutes) and immediate notification, which is crucial for responding swiftly to sudden traffic increases.

Launch an Amazon EMR job that runs on a 30-minute interval, parses CloudWatch application logs from the EC2 instances in your Auto Scaling group, analyzes them for traffic spikes, and then uses Amazon SNS to send SMS alerts to your team is inefficient for timely detection, as it introduces delays due to batch processing and is more complex to maintain.

Create a CloudWatch alarm tracking the NetworkOut metric of your Application Load Balancer and configure Amazon SNS to instantly notify your team if a predefined threshold is crossed focuses on outbound traffic from the load balancer, whereas the objective is to identify incoming traffic surges to your instances.

Schedule a periodic Amazon EMR analysis every 30 minutes to batch process access logs from the Application Load Balancer, and utilize Amazon Simple Email Service for team notifications if unusual traffic is detected also suffers from delayed batch processing and adds unnecessary complexity.

Configure a cron task to continuously inspect AWS CloudTrail for events indicating high traffic and employ Amazon SNS to deliver alerts to your team is not ideal because CloudTrail primarily logs API activity, not network-level traffic. This would not provide accurate insights on real-time traffic surges.

Tip: For operational monitoring of traffic, always leverage CloudWatch metrics and alarms for real-time alerting. Choose metrics like NetworkIn or relevant request count metrics for timely detection of load changes. SNS offers seamless integration for alerts to email, SMS, or other endpoints.
