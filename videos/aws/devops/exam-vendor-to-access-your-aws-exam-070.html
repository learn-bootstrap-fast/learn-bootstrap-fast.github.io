The cross-account IAM Role method is the most secure and recommended solution for granting a vendor temporary and limited access to your S3 bucket. By creating a Role with the minimum necessary permissions and allowing it to be assumed by the vendor's AWS account, you avoid long-term shared credentials and maintain strong control and auditing capability.

The option Set up an IAM User with API keys, provide necessary permissions, then share the access key and secret with the vendor is risky because distributing IAM user credentials breaks the principle of least privilege, makes rotation harder, and can expose your account to unneeded risk if keys are compromised.

Provision an EC2 instance with an instance profile that has full access to the bucket, and grant SSH access on the server to the vendor is overly complex, introduces unnecessary infrastructure, and poses additional security challenges without cleanly controlling access just to S3.

Creating and sharing pre-signed S3 PUT and GET URLs with wildcards and two-year durations is not appropriate, as presigned URLs are best used for short-term limited access, not for vendor solutions requiring ongoing controlled integration.

Tip: Always leverage IAM Roles for cross-account access when a third party with their own AWS account needs access. This maximizes security, auditing, and flexibility while following AWS best practices.
