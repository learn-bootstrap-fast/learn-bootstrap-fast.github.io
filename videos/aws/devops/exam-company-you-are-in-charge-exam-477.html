The correct answer is Elastic Load Balancer access logging is not enabled by default and needs to be manually configured.

Explanation: ELB access logs are a feature that must be explicitly activated; by default, logging is turned off. If you have not enabled access logging, the logs will not show up in the designated S3 bucket, which explains why you do not see any recorded files.

  • The EC2 instances within the environment are failing to generate or transmit the required access logs to ELB. — This is incorrect because ELB access logs are produced by the ELB service itself, not by EC2 instances.
  • You lack the necessary permissions to view the log files produced by the ELB. — Although permissions can restrict your ability to access the logs, the scenario specifically notes that no logs have been recorded at all, which suggests a configuration issue rather than a permissions one.
  • The Auto Scaling group is not forwarding needed logs to the ELB for storage. — The Auto Scaling group is not responsible for sending logs to the ELB. Log generation and storage are managed by the ELB service once access logging is enabled.

Tip: Always remember to verify ELB access log configuration when troubleshooting missing logs. Ensure the S3 bucket is also correctly specified, and that the ELB has permissions to publish to it.
