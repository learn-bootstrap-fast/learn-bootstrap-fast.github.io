The correct approach in this scenario is to Increase the CloudWatch alarm period that activates your Auto Scaling scale-down action. By doing this, you provide a longer observation window before triggering a scale-down event, which reduces the likelihood of the Auto Scaling group reacting to temporary or short-lived spikes. This helps avoid frequent, rapid scaling in and out (also known as 'flapping') and leads to more stable resource allocation—thus better controlling costs.

Adjusting the Auto Scaling policy to implement scheduled scaling, based on known workload patterns is effective only if your traffic is reliably predictable and follows a regular schedule; it does not prevent rapid scaling in response to unpredictable events, so it may not address the flapping issue here.

Alter the cooldown timer settings for the Auto Scaling Group is related, but cooldowns are meant to prevent launching or terminating multiple instances in quick succession after an action. While useful, an extended alarm period gives more direct control over how quickly the scaling policies trigger.

Change the group’s termination policy to always remove the latest launched instance first has no effect on reducing scale activity frequency—it only affects which instance is chosen when terminating, not how often scaling happens.

Tip: To avoid unnecessary scaling actions due to short-lived spikes, always consider tuning the monitoring period of your CloudWatch alarms and review your scaling thresholds to align with real-world application behavior.
