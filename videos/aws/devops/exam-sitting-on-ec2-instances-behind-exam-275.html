With the application behind a Load Balancer utilizing an Auto Scaling group, establish a fresh Auto Scaling group and attach it to the load balancer. After verifying the new environment is operational, reduce the desired capacity of the original group to zero, and remove the old group once the instances have terminated. best exemplifies the Blue-Green deployment pattern on AWS. This approach creates a parallel 'green' environment for the new version, seamlessly attaches it to the existing Load Balancer, and shifts user traffic only after full verification, minimizing downtime and deployment risk. Once everything checks out, the 'blue' (old) infrastructure is retired cleanly.

The other options are not fully accurate for Blue-Green deployment:
Leverage AWS CloudFormation by maintaining two separate stacks for your resources and switch to the alternative stack as needed by redeploying does not specifically describe traffic switching via a load balancer, and redeploying can lead to more downtime compared to real Blue-Green deployments.
Use AWS Elastic Beanstalk to manage two separate environments and leverage Rolling deployments to alternate between versions refers to Rolling deployments, which upgrade instances in place and don't provide completely separate environments for instant switching, as Blue-Green does.
Utilize AWS OpsWorks to set up two layers in a stack for two distinct application versions, and perform the deployment switch by toggling between the layers uses the layering concept, but OpsWorks is less commonly used for Blue-Green, and layers are not meant for environment switching in this manner.

Tip: A true Blue-Green deployment requires two parallel, identical environments, allowing near-instant traffic switch and easy rollback.
