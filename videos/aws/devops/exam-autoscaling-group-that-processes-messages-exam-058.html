The correct answer is Implement lifecycle hooks so that instance termination waits until processing tasks are complete. Lifecycle hooks pause the Auto Scaling termination process, giving your EC2 instance extra time to complete any remaining workloads (such as finishing video processing) and perform cleanup before actually shutting down. This mechanism ensures that no tasks are left incomplete or lost when scaling in occurs.

Extend the cooldown period for the Auto Scaling Group to delay additional scaling activities is incorrect because cooldowns control the frequency of scaling actions, but do not prevent termination of an instance that is still processing a job. Cooldowns simply avoid rapid scaling actions, not the in-flight task scenario.

Inactivate the AZRebalance policy to stop redistribution of instances across Availability Zones is not relevant, because the AZRebalance termination policy concerns balancing the distribution of instances among availability zones and does not resolve the problem of tasks being interrupted by scale-in actions.

Increase the min and max instance counts in the Auto Scaling Group and reduce dynamic scaling adjustments could make scale-in events less frequent, but it does not guarantee that termination won’t happen while a job is in progress. It would also result in inefficient resource usage and higher costs.

Tip: For workloads that require graceful shutdown or need to safely complete in-process tasks before termination, always employ lifecycle hooks with Auto Scaling. Also, update your application logic to report completion back to the hook so the instance can be safely terminated by Auto Scaling.
