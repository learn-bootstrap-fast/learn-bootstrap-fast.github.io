The ideal solution is AWS Service Catalog. This service lets you centrally curate, manage, and distribute portfolios of approved products, which can consist of CloudFormation stacks, and control who in your organization can provision them. It enables you to assign granular access to different teams using IAM roles and groups—ensuring only authorized users can create, update, or delete resources as appropriate.

Why not AWS CloudFormation? While CloudFormation helps you model and set up AWS resources, it doesn't provide built-in access controls or a product-based management portal for delegation and restrictions across teams.
Why not AWS Trusted Advisor? Trusted Advisor is a tool for cost optimization, best practices, and security checks, but it doesn’t help with provisioning or access management for CloudFormation products.
Why not AWS Systems Manager? Systems Manager is designed for operational management, automation, and patching, not specifically for cataloging and controlled provisioning of CloudFormation stacks.

Tip: Use Service Catalog when you need catalog-based provisioning and fine-grained, team-based access to infrastructure as code in AWS.
