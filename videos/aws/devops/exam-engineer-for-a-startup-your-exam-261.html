The best approach is Dividing the stack into three CloudFormation templates for data, compute, and network layers and applying Blue-Green deployment with automation. Blue-Green deployments significantly lower the risk of production failures by providing a quick and reliable rollback mechanism—you can direct traffic to the existing environment if issues arise after deployment. Structuring your stack into separate templates allows for clear resource boundaries, improved reusability, and easier troubleshooting, as well as enforcing dependency ordering during deployment.

The other options have notable shortcomings:

  • Organize the infrastructure in AWS Elastic Beanstalk as a single application with separate environments, and implement Rolling Deploys to progressively deploy changes across environments. — While Elastic Beanstalk supports rolling deployments, it doesn't offer granular control over individual infrastructure components and isn't ideal for complex multi-layer stacks.
  • Set up AWS OpsWorks as a single stack, containing one compute layer and its corresponding Elastic Load Balancer. — This lacks modular separation between resources. Managing everything within a single stack restricts isolation and flexibility, increasing risk when deploying changes.
  • Leverage Chef and Application Deployments to set up and automate rolling application deployments. — Chef is powerful for configuration management, but this answer lacks clarity on managing environments and does not address stack-level risk mitigation, such as isolation or rollback strategies.
  • Create the entire infrastructure in a single CloudFormation template and automate deployment and integration tests with Rolling Deployment strategies. — A single, large template is harder to maintain and troubleshoot. Modular templates improve clarity and resilience, especially for large stacks.

Key Tip: Use multiple CloudFormation templates to clearly separate and manage different parts of your stack, and choose Blue-Green deployment methodology for safer, low-risk deployments, particularly in production environments.
