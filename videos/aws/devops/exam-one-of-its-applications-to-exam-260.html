The correct recovery steps in a warm-standby disaster recovery architecture are:

  • Leverage Amazon Route 53 to reroute all client requests to the environment in ap-southeast-2. Route 53 enables DNS failover, which allows user traffic to be directed to the backup environment when the primary region fails.
  • Scale the Auto Scaling groups in the secondary region to efficiently handle the increased user load. In a warm-standby setup, the standby environment often runs at minimal capacity to save cost. During failover, it must be scaled up to meet production demand.

Frequently use EBS Lifecycle Manager to schedule and take EBS volume snapshots is incorrect because this action helps protect data, but it is not part of the immediate recovery steps needed during failover—these snapshots are more about maintaining backups.

Backup necessary data sets to Amazon S3 and activate encryption for confidential information relates to data durability and security, but does not address the actual process of switching operations to the standby region after a failure.

Produce custom Amazon Machine Images (AMIs) and launch the application on new EC2 instances is not required for warm-standby, since this architecture presumes that the secondary environment is already provisioned and ready to operate; launching new instances from AMIs is more relevant for cold standby scenarios.

Tip: The most important actions during failover are to redirect traffic and ensure the standby environment is fully operational and capable of handling normal traffic volumes.
