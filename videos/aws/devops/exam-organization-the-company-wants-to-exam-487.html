Partition templates by function, such as having distinct templates for networking, application, and security setups, and incorporate nesting to link them appropriately. is the best approach. By modularizing CloudFormation templates—creating logically separated ones for different domains like networking, security, and application layers—you ensure each template remains maintainable, reusable, and easier for respective teams to update independently. Nested stacks (using the AWS::CloudFormation::Stack resource) can orchestrate dependencies and combine these focused templates into a full solution.

Why not the others?

  • Use a single comprehensive CloudFormation template to lower maintenance work across teams: Large monolithic templates are difficult to manage, version control, and collaborate on, especially when requirements change—a common scenario in large organizations with multiple teams.
  • Leverage Elastic Beanstalk for environment creation, as CloudFormation cannot handle this level of customization: Elastic Beanstalk is a platform-as-a-service solution ideal for simple app deployments, but it lacks the flexibility and depth of resource provisioning and customization that CloudFormation provides, especially when creating complex, cross-cutting resources for an organization.
  • Switch to AWS OpsWorks for provisioning as CloudFormation isn’t suited for large-scale customization: OpsWorks is generally used for configuration management and orchestrating deployments using Chef/Puppet-like stacks, and doesn't directly provide the same deep customization and IaC capabilities as CloudFormation.

Tip: Use nested stacks to manage dependencies between teams while allowing each department to maintain its area of responsibility. This also makes updates and error tracking much easier!
