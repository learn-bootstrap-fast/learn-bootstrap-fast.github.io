Persist log data in Amazon S3, implementing lifecycle policies to automatically transition older logs to Amazon Glacier. is the most suitable and cost-optimized approach. Storing logs in Amazon S3 provides highly durable, scalable, and readily accessible storage for recent logs, allowing fast retrieval for operational troubleshooting. Lifecycle policies simplify the management of long-term retention by automatically transitioning less frequently accessed logs to lower-cost storage classes such as Glacier, which is ideal for archival needs spanning 10 years.

Utilize Amazon Glacier for log storage. is incorrect because Glacier is designed for archiving and offers very slow retrieval, making it impractical when there is a requirement for prompt access to newer logs.

Send your logs to Amazon CloudWatch Logs. does not support cost-effective long-term archival of large log volumes over a decade, as retention costs and scalability become less ideal over time, especially for very high log volume scenarios.

Save logs on Amazon EBS volumes and regularly create Amazon EBS snapshots for archiving. is not the optimal solution because EBS volumes and their snapshots are primarily intended for block storage tied to EC2 instances. This approach would be significantly more expensive at scale, less durable than S3, and more challenging to automate and retrieve individual log files over a 10-year period.

  • Tip: Use S3 for high durability and easy integration with other AWS services. Lifecycle policies help reduce cost by automatically moving data to more suitable storage tiers over time.
