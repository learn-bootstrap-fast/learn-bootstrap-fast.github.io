To protect data while it is transmitted between clients and your AWS Elastic Load Balancer (ELB), encryption via SSL/TLS is essential. This is achieved by using either a SSL listener or an HTTPS listener on your ELB’s front end, as both encrypt the data during transit, helping to prevent interception and eavesdropping.

Options such as \"Set up your Elastic Load Balancer to accept HTTP connections on the front end.\" and \"Choose to use a TCP front end listener when configuring your Elastic Load Balancer.\" do not provide encryption by default. HTTP is plain text and not secure, while TCP listeners may be required for certain applications but do not themselves provide encryption unless paired with an encrypted protocol or service.

Key tip: Always prefer using SSL or HTTPS listeners on your ELB for encrypted communication. While TCP and HTTP listeners may be needed for certain scenarios, they do not inherently secure data in transit. When compliance or security is a concern, deploying SSL/TLS is the recommended approach.
