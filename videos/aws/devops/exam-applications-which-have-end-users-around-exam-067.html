The Weighted policy for both Blue/Green and Canary deployments, and Failover policy for Disaster Recovery is the best match for the routing needs described.

  • Blue/Green deployment typically requires managing how much traffic is directed to the old (Blue) and new (Green) environments. The weighted routing policy allows you to smoothly shift traffic between two different resource sets by adjusting weights.
  • Canary deployment benefits from the Weighted policy as well, since you can direct a small percentage of user requests to the new version before gradually increasing the traffic—ensuring smooth rollouts with less risk.
  • Disaster Recovery is best handled with the Failover policy, which routes traffic to a primary resource, but automatically shifts to a backup destination if the primary becomes unhealthy, supporting robust DR strategies.

The other options are incorrect:

  • Weighted policy for Blue/Green deployment, Latency policy for Canary deployment, and Failover policy for Disaster Recovery: Latency-based routing is not suitable for Canary deployments, as it doesn’t provide granular control of traffic percentages.
  • Simple policy for Blue/Green deployment, Weighted policy for Canary deployment, and Latency policy for Disaster Recovery: Simple routing cannot help with traffic shifting needed for Blue/Green, and Latency policy is not for DR situations.
  • Latency policy for Blue/Green deployment, Simple policy for Canary deployment, and Weighted policy for Disaster Recovery: Latency and simple policies do not give the control necessary for deployment strategies, and weighted routing is not for disaster recovery scenarios; failover is the better choice.

Tip: Use Weighted policies for progressive rollouts and deployments (Blue/Green, Canary), and Failover for high availability and DR setups in Route 53.
